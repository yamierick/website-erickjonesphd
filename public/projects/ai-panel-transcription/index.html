AI Panel: Live Transcription and Images from a Discussion

Software that listens to a panel discussion, transcribes it as people speak, and turns what they say into images, summaries and speaking-time charts, all on one laptop.

  • Active
  • 2025–present
  • Speech recognition
  • Generative AI
  • Local AI
  • Events

In plain English

Panel discussions are hard to follow and harder to remember. This project asks whether a live discussion can be captured as it happens and turned into something an audience can see. Small cooperating programs do the work: one listens to the microphone and cuts the audio at pauses, one transcribes it with an open speech-recognition model, Whisper, and one asks a local language model to turn the latest sentences into a short image prompt, which an open image model then draws. A dashboard shows the running transcript, the newest image, who has spoken for how long, the most repeated words, and a summary at the end.

Everything runs on a single laptop with no cloud services, so nothing said in the room leaves the machine. Students rebuilt the pipeline several times, from a browser-and-cloud prototype in 2025 to a fully local version tuned for an ordinary laptop graphics card in 2026. One fix along the way, switching off the language model's step-by-step "thinking" mode, cut each image prompt from a minute or more to about 3 seconds. The newest version follows two microphones and switches to whoever is speaking loudest. Separating every speaker on a full panel is next.

Main points

  • Transcribes speech in real time with an open Whisper model, cut at natural pauses.
  • A local language model turns recent speech into image prompts, and an open image model draws them.
  • The dashboard tracks speaking time, repeated words and an end-of-session summary, and exports a PDF.
  • Runs entirely on one laptop: no cloud services, and no data leaves the room.
  • Next: telling multiple speakers apart, and capturing a whole room.
